## 1. What Gehler's answer actually changes

His first answer is not a nicety — it moves the project's **outcome variable**.

Everything measured so far (recall, precision, steering, thesis-signal accuracy) measures
*artifact quality*: did the tool name the right chairs. Gehler grades *user change*: did
the student end up with a sharper understanding of the thesis they want. Those are
different variables, and the second one has never been measured here even once.

Three consequences, all inside the chosen scope (**reframe + measure, no skill rewrite**):

1. **G3's value proposition is rewritten.** Not "a more accurate chair finder than plain
   Claude" but: *a structured conversation that forces a student to articulate interests,
   methods, no-gos and thesis style, and then confronts that articulation with real,
   verified options — so the student leaves knowing better what they want, whether or not
   they contact anyone on the list.* This framing is not retrofitted spin: the six-dimension
   profile gate, the one-question interview discipline, the honest-caveat output, and the
   "no fit found" rule were all built to serve exactly this, and the project's own edge-case
   Task R (niche no-match → honest "no strong fit", no padding) only makes sense under it.
2. **The beta protocol gets a reflection instrument** (Task AC′): a pre/post self-statement
   — the tester writes 2 sentences on "what kind of thesis am I looking for" *before* the
   run and again *after*, and both are kept verbatim. Change between the two is the primary
   outcome; finding an unknown option becomes secondary. This is cheap, it directly
   operationalises what the grader said he values, and n=2–3 is enough for a qualitative
   claim if the statements are quoted rather than scored.
3. **The simulation rubric gets an eighth dimension** (Task AL): *reflection support* — did
   the run leave the (simulated) student with a sharper articulation of what they want, not
   just a list. Adding it before the evidence run means the current architecture is measured
   on the graded variable from the start.

**Not changed:** the discovery skills themselves. Recall/precision stay as supporting
evidence — they are the reason to trust the options that trigger the reflection. Turning
the tool into a primarily reflective instrument would discard most existing evidence for no
rubric gain, and was explicitly rejected on 2026-08-08.

**The reframing is not opportunistic — three independent signals converge on it**, and this
is the strongest single narrative thread available to the report:

1. **May 2026, the professor interviews.** "Bob" reframed the data model away from topic
   inventories and drafted disclaimer language the research package recommended adopting
   nearly verbatim: topics are *"starting points only; an essential part of doing a thesis
   is the formulation and refinement of the problem itself"*
   (`docs/thesis-report/00-problem-and-research/2026-06-12-professor-research-package/detailed_analysis.md`).
2. **August 2026, Gehler.** The value is that the student reflected and sharpened their
   own understanding — not the proposals.
3. **August 2026, the team's own instinct.** Valentin's AI-disclaimer rule (§0.4) tells the
   student to rewrite the draft, cut what they cannot defend, and verify every claim — the
   same principle applied at the outreach step, arrived at independently.

Three sources, three months apart, saying that the artifact is a starting point and the
student's own thinking is the product. The project acted on this repeatedly without ever
stating it as the value proposition. Task AH states it.

His other three points map to **three new tasks that did not exist in any previous plan**:
AM (reconstructed user-study chapter), AN (future-feedback plan), AO (survival plan). All
three are explicitly invited by the grader, so they are cheap points that are currently at
zero.

## 2. The evidence break — the central honesty problem of this plan

**Every hard number this project has was produced by an architecture that no longer exists.**

| Evidence | Produced | Architecture | Still valid? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Recall CS 100%, Med ≥83%, Psych ≥83%, WiSo ≥83%, Humanities 100%, Law 80% | 2026-06-28 … 2026-07-04 | backbone-crawl | **Measures the deleted architecture** |
| Precision CS 100% (Task O), Law 100%, Humanities 100% | 2026-07-02 … 07-04 | backbone-crawl | same |
| Steering proof (Task P, personas A/B near-disjoint) | 2026-07-02 | backbone-crawl | Mechanism claim probably survives (steering comes from profile→query mapping, which the pivot kept), but untested post-pivot |
| Company recall 100% / +26pp (Task 2-E) | 2026-06-28 | BW backbone | Doubly dead: circular *and* the backbone is deleted |
| +65pp live delta over plain Claude (Task I) | 2026-06-28 | backbone-crawl | The strongest comparative number the project has — and it belongs to the old architecture |
| Simulation before/after: baseline mean 20.0 → rules-only 20.75, 6/6 gates passed, 4 comparable personas | 2026-07-31 … 08-04 | **rules-only vs. backbone** | **Valid and directly on point — but it lives only in the PR #71 description; the artifacts are gitignored and were deliberately not committed** |
| Four new personas at 20/21, evidence discipline 3/3 | 2026-07-31 | rules-only | Same: PR description only, no artifact |

So the accurate statement is not "no post-pivot evidence" — the 2026-07-30 follow-up
("compare Jan, Simon, Maja, Tina against a fresh `current main` baseline") **was actually
run, and it passed.** The problems are narrower and fixable:

1. **The evidence is not in the repo.** A GitHub PR description is not a citable artifact
   for a submission, and it disappears from view the moment someone reads the repo alone.
2. **It measures a different thing than the pre-pivot numbers.** The rubric scores
   conversation quality 0–3 across seven dimensions; the Phase-4 numbers are recall against
   curated ground truth. "20.0 → 20.75" cannot answer "does rules-only still find Remmert."
   The two are not comparable, and the report must not present them as a continuous series.
3. **It is self-scored.** The same model family generated the assistant turns, played the
   student, and graded the result. The black-box harness split is a genuine mitigation of
   *leakage*, not of *scoring bias*.

So: the shipping architecture has one good, uncommitted, self-scored conversation-quality
result and **zero ground-truth measurements**. Tasks AL (commit and re-run the simulation
evidence) and AK (add the ground-truth measurement) close exactly those two gaps.

Three ways out; this plan takes the third:

- *(a)* Re-run everything post-pivot — too expensive, and the eval ground truth was built for
  the backbone architecture's failure modes.
- *(b)* Report the old numbers and stay quiet about the pivot — indefensible, and the repo's
  own precedent (the 2026-06-28 premature-GREEN incident, the 2026-07-05 provisional-GO
  flag) is to catch exactly this.
- **(c) Label every pre-pivot number as pre-pivot, re-measure a deliberate subset post-pivot,
  and bring the simulation evidence into the repo.** The architecture change then becomes an
  *asset* for G1 ("problem framing and evolution" now has three dated evolution steps, not
  two) and for G5 ("testing approach" includes catching that one's own evidence had expired,
  and knowing which of two measurement instruments can answer which question).

Tasks **AJ** (audit/label), **V′** + **AK** (ground-truth re-measurement), **AL** (commit
the simulation evidence) implement (c). They are the blocking prerequisites for the
benchmark chapter.

**The specific question AK must answer.** The pivot cut `search-strategy.md` from 461 lines
to 52 (§0.1). Task U's enrich-before-exclude rule survived as a single sentence, but the
Remmert worked example that demonstrated it did not. Remmert was the chair whose dense
multi-strand German title ("Staats- und Verwaltungsrecht, Öffentliches Wirtschaftsrecht,
Kommunalrecht") reads economic/municipal on its surface and who only surfaces if the model
enriches before judging — she is the single data point that flipped the Phase-4 verdict to
GO. **Does a one-line rule without its worked example still find her?** The simulation
rubric cannot answer that; only a ground-truth run can. If AK's answer is no, that is not a
disaster — it is the most interesting empirical result the project will produce, and it
says something real about how much of an LLM skill's behaviour lives in its examples rather
than its rules.
