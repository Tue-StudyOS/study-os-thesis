- **⚠ Robustness finding — vacancies make this a genuinely chair-poor faculty for some interests (the Task-P caveat, live).** Protestant Theology lists chairs almost directly (structurally *easy*), but several Lehrstühle are currently **N.N. (vacant)**: Altes Testament I, Neues Testament II (Evangelienforschung), Kirchenordnung/Neuere Kirchengeschichte, **Systematische Theologie II (Ethik und Christliche Gesellschaftslehre)**, Systematische Theologie III (Fundamentaltheologie/Religionsphilosophie), and Praktische Theologie II (Religionspädagogik). This is exactly the chair-scarcity condition Task P flagged (steering-proof caveat #3): for a student whose interest is **theological ethics** or **fundamental theology / philosophy of religion**, the directly-relevant chair is *vacant right now*, so an honest discovery run should report "no currently-staffed chair for this exact focus at this faculty" rather than route them to a filled but off-topic chair. **Record any such outcome as a robustness/coverage result, not a recall failure** — the ground truth for the biblical-studies sample interest above is well-staffed precisely to keep this file's recall metric clean; the vacancy point is documented here as the hard-faculty finding.
- **Steering implication.** Because the *staffed* chairs cluster in biblical studies + church history, two personas that both fall inside that cluster (e.g. an OT and an NT student) can still diverge (Leuenberger/Kamlah vs. Tilly/Landmesser), but a systematic-theology vs. biblical-studies split would show one side with **few or no staffed chairs** — a robustness limit on steering at a small faculty, not a mechanism failure.
